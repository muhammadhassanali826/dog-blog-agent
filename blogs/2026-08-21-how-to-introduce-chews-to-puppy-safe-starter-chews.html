Why Do Puppies Need Chews?

Puppies explore the world with their mouths, and chewing is a natural, instinctual behavior. It serves several crucial purposes during their development:

  • Teething Relief: Just like human babies, puppies experience discomfort as their adult teeth come in. Chewing can help soothe their sore gums.
  • Mental Stimulation: Chewing provides an outlet for mental energy, helping to prevent boredom and destructive behaviors. A bored puppy is often a mischievous puppy!
  • Dental Health: Regular chewing can help clean teeth, reduce plaque and tartar buildup, and support gum health.
  • Instinctual Satisfaction: Chewing is deeply ingrained in a dog's nature. Providing appropriate outlets satisfies this primal need.

When Can You Start Introducing Chews?

You can begin introducing safe chews to your puppy as soon as they come home, provided they are age-appropriate and supervised. For very young puppies (under 8 weeks), soft, edible treats are usually best. As they grow and their chewing habits develop, you can introduce more durable options.

Choosing the Right Starter Chews

Not all chews are created equal, especially for a developing puppy. When selecting starter chews, consider these factors:

Digestibility:

Puppies have sensitive digestive systems. Opt for chews made from natural, single-ingredient proteins that are easily digestible. Avoid artificial additives, fillers, and excessive processing.

Size and Shape:

The chew should be appropriately sized for your puppy to handle without being a choking hazard. It shouldn't be so small that they can swallow it whole, nor so large that they become frustrated.

Durability:

For young puppies, softer chews are often better. As they get older and their jaws strengthen, you can introduce slightly more durable options. Always avoid very hard chews that could damage delicate puppy teeth.

Safety:

Look for rawhide-free options. Rawhide can pose choking and blockage risks. Avoid chews with added sugars, salts, or artificial ingredients.

How to Safely Introduce Your Puppy to Chews

The key to a successful puppy chew introduction is supervision and patience. Here’s a step-by-step approach:

Step 1: Choose the Right Chew

Start with a smaller, softer, or more digestible chew like a Brutus & Barnaby Bully Stick. Ensure it's sized correctly for your puppy. For training or initial introductions, smaller training treats can also be useful.

Step 2: Supervise Closely

Never leave your puppy unattended with a chew, especially when first introducing it. Watch for any signs of distress, choking, or aggressive chewing. Ensure they are gnawing and not trying to swallow large pieces.

Step 3: Observe Chewing Style

Pay attention to how your puppy interacts with the chew. Are they gently gnawing, or are they a power chewer who tries to break off large chunks? This will help you select appropriate chews in the future.

Step 4: Introduce Gradually

Offer the chew for short periods initially, perhaps 10-15 minutes. You can take it away and offer it again later. This helps prevent overexcitement and ensures they don't consume too much at once.

Step 5: Know When to Take It Away

Remove the chew when it becomes small enough to swallow whole, or if your puppy starts trying to break off large pieces. This is crucial for preventing choking hazards.

What to Avoid When Introducing Puppy Chews

Certain chews and practices can be harmful or counterproductive for puppies. Steer clear of:

  • Rawhide: Can cause blockages and choking.
  • Cooked Bones: Prone to splintering, which can cause internal damage.
  • Very Hard Chews: Like antlers or hard nylon bones, which can fracture puppy teeth.
  • Chews with Artificial Ingredients: Avoid anything with artificial colors, flavors, or preservatives.
  • Unsupervised Chewing: Never leave a puppy alone with a chew.
  • Overfeeding: Chews are treats and should be given in moderation as part of a balanced diet. Consult resources like AKC: How many treats can a dog have? for guidance.

A Note on Moderation and Diet

It's important to remember that chews are treats and should be factored into your puppy's overall caloric intake. Giving too many treats can lead to weight gain and nutritional imbalances. Always ensure your puppy has access to fresh water. For more on treat moderation, the VCA Hospitals: Dog treats and moderation offers valuable insights.

When to Consult Your Veterinarian

While chewing is natural and beneficial, always monitor your puppy for any signs of trouble. If you notice any of the following, consult your veterinarian immediately:

  • Choking or gagging during chewing
  • Signs of digestive upset (vomiting, diarrhea)
  • Sudden changes in appetite or thirst
  • Excessive drooling or pawing at the mouth
  • Lethargy or unusual behavior

Your vet can provide personalized advice based on your puppy's specific health needs and chewing habits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Can I give my 8-week-old puppy a bully stick?

Yes, you can introduce a bully stick to an 8-week-old puppy, but it's crucial to choose a size appropriate for their small mouth and to supervise them constantly. Start with shorter chew sessions. Always ensure it's a natural, single-ingredient bully stick from a reputable source like Brutus & Barnaby.

Q2: How long should a puppy chew on a bully stick?

For initial introductions, limit chew sessions to 10-20 minutes. As your puppy gets used to it and you're confident in their chewing habits, you can gradually increase the time. Always remove the bully stick when it becomes small enough to swallow whole.

Q3: What are the best rawhide alternatives for puppies?

Excellent rawhide alternatives for puppies include natural bully sticks, cow ears, pig ears (given their size and chewing style), and dental chews specifically designed for puppies. These options are generally more digestible and less prone to causing blockages.

Q4: How do I know if a chew is too hard for my puppy?

If a chew is too hard, it might make a loud clacking sound when your puppy chews it, or they might struggle to make any impression on it. Chews that are harder than your fingernail can potentially fracture puppy teeth. Stick to more pliable options like bully sticks or softer natural chews until their adult teeth are fully in and their jaw is stronger.

Q5: Should I worry about my puppy eating the chew?

Yes, you should always monitor your puppy to ensure they are safely gnawing and not trying to swallow large pieces. Remove the chew when it becomes a choking hazard. If your puppy has a sensitive stomach or a history of digestive issues, discuss chew choices with your vet.

Q6: How often can I give my puppy chews?

Chews should be considered treats. Aim to give them in moderation, perhaps a few times a week, as part of a balanced diet. Over-gifting chews can lead to weight gain or digestive upset. Always factor them into your puppy's daily caloric intake, and consult your vet if you're unsure about frequency.
