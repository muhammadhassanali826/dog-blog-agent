Quick Answer:

The safest chews for puppies are typically digestible, natural options like small bully sticks, natural rawhide alternatives (e.g., beef tendons, collagen chews), and durable rubber chew toys designed for puppies. Always supervise your puppy with any chew and ensure it’s appropriately sized.

Why Do Puppies Need Chews?

As any puppy parent knows, puppies are bundles of energy and curiosity. A significant part of their exploration involves their mouths. Chewing is a natural and essential behavior for puppies for several reasons:

  • Teething Relief: Just like human babies, puppies experience discomfort as their baby teeth fall out and adult teeth emerge. Chewing helps to alleviate this gum soreness and pressure.
  • Dental Health: Chewing can help to clean teeth and gums, reducing the risk of plaque and tartar buildup.
  • Mental Stimulation & Boredom Busting: A bored puppy can quickly become a mischievous puppy. Offering appropriate chews provides mental engagement and helps prevent destructive chewing on furniture or shoes.
  • Training Aid: Chews can be used as rewards during training sessions, helping to reinforce good behavior.
  • Digestive Health: Some natural chews can aid in digestion and provide essential nutrients.

What Makes a Chew “Safe” for Puppies?

When choosing safe puppy treats and chews, consider these key factors:

1. Digestibility

The most crucial aspect of chew safety is digestibility. If a chew breaks off into large pieces that can be swallowed whole, it poses a choking hazard or can cause gastrointestinal blockages. Natural, edible chews are generally preferred over hard plastics or extremely dense materials that can chip teeth.

2. Size and Shape

A chew should be large enough that your puppy cannot swallow it whole, but not so large that it becomes unmanageable. Avoid anything that could get lodged in their throat or become a choking hazard. Regularly inspect chews and discard them when they become small enough to be a risk.

3. Material and Ingredients

Opt for natural, high-quality ingredients. Avoid chews with artificial colors, flavors, or preservatives. Rawhide, while popular, can be problematic for some puppies due to its digestibility issues and potential for splintering. Many owners seek safer alternatives.

4. Hardness

Extremely hard chews, like some nylon bones or antlers, can be too tough for a puppy’s developing teeth and may cause fractures. Chews should be firm enough to withstand chewing but have a slight give.

5. Supervision

No chew is completely risk-free. Always supervise your puppy when they are enjoying a new chew, especially during their first few times. This allows you to monitor their chewing style and intervene if any issues arise.

Chews to Approach with Caution or Avoid for Puppies

Not all chews are created equal, and some can pose significant risks to young puppies:

1. Rawhide

While a common choice, rawhide can be difficult for some dogs to digest and can expand in the stomach, potentially causing blockages. It can also break into large, indigestible chunks. If you choose rawhide, opt for high-quality, natural options and supervise VERY closely. Many owners prefer to avoid it altogether for puppies.

2. Hard Antlers, Bones, and Nylon Chews

These can be too hard for a puppy's delicate teeth and may lead to fractured teeth, which are painful and require veterinary intervention. The risk of tooth damage often outweighs the benefit of a long-lasting chew for a young puppy.

3. Cooked Bones

Cooked bones, regardless of the animal, can splinter easily and pose a severe choking hazard or internal injury risk. Always avoid giving cooked bones to your puppy.

4. Tennis Balls and Small Toys

These can be easily swallowed whole or in pieces, leading to choking or intestinal blockages. Even if they are larger, supervise your puppy to ensure they don't break off pieces they can ingest.

How to Introduce New Chews Safely

Introducing a new chew is an exciting time, but safety should always be the top priority. Follow these steps:

  1. Choose the Right Chew: Select a chew that is age-appropriate, sized correctly, and made from safe, digestible ingredients.
  2. Supervise Closely: Especially the first few times, stay with your puppy and monitor their chewing.
  3. Observe Chewing Habits: Does your puppy try to swallow large pieces? Are they gnawing aggressively, or gently?
  4. Introduce Gradually: Don't leave a new chew with your puppy unsupervised for extended periods initially.
  5. Provide Variety (Wisely): Rotate different types of safe chews to keep things interesting and cater to different chewing needs. You can learn more about this in our Dog Treat Rotation Guide.
  6. Know When to Discard: Once a chew becomes small enough to be swallowed whole or poses a choking risk, it's time to throw it away.

A Note on Treat Moderation

While chews are great, they should be given in moderation. Treats, including chews, should not make up more than 10% of your puppy’s daily caloric intake. Overfeeding treats can lead to obesity and nutritional imbalances. Always consider your puppy’s overall diet and consult your veterinarian if you have concerns about treat quantity or your puppy's weight. Frequently Asked Questions About Puppy Chews

Are bully sticks safe for puppies?

Yes, bully sticks are generally considered safe and highly digestible for puppies, provided they are appropriately sized and supervised. They are a great natural alternative to rawhide.

How long should a puppy chew on a bully stick?

Puppies can chew on bully sticks for varying lengths of time depending on their age, breed, and chewing intensity. It’s common for a puppy to spend 15-30 minutes on a bully stick, but this can vary. Always supervise and remove the bully stick when it becomes small enough to swallow.

What is the safest chew for a teething puppy?

For teething puppies, soft rubber toys or highly digestible, natural chews like small bully sticks or cow ears are often the safest. These provide relief without posing a high risk of tooth damage or choking. Always supervise.

Can puppies have rawhide?

Rawhide is a controversial chew for puppies. While some puppies can handle it, it poses risks like choking, blockages, and digestive upset due to its indigestible nature. Many veterinarians and trainers recommend avoiding rawhide for puppies in favor of safer alternatives.

How often can I give my puppy a chew?

Chews should be given in moderation, as part of a balanced diet. They should not exceed 10% of your puppy’s daily caloric intake. Offering a chew a few times a week, or as a special reward, is generally appropriate. Always monitor for any digestive upset.

What should I do if my puppy swallows a large piece of a chew?

If your puppy swallows a large piece of a chew, monitor them closely for any signs of distress, choking, vomiting, diarrhea, or lethargy. Contact your veterinarian immediately if you notice any concerning symptoms or suspect a blockage.
